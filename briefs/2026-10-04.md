## Regressions and risks

**1. The same blog post has been written twice.** `26c70f9` (20 Sept, slug
`how-to-write-a-linkedin-summary-that-gets-noticed`, "…That Actually Gets Noticed") and `4ef92fa`
(27 Sept, slug `how-to-write-a-linkedin-summary`, "…That Gets Noticed") are both LinkedIn-summary
posts on separate branches, written a week apart, each described in its own commit message as
rotating the blog toward working professionals. The second was almost certainly written because
the first was invisible on `main`. This is the unmerged backlog costing new work rather than just
delaying old work, and it is the concrete form of the waste the earlier briefs warned about.
Merging both would put two near-identical posts on the site competing for one query. **Pick one,
merge it, delete the other branch.**

**2. `hero-redraft` is now superseded and conflicting.** Four conflict hunks against current
`main`. It was recommended last week for a read-through-then-merge; the 30 Sept hero rewrite
overtook it. Lift out the parts still wanted (`EmailCapture.tsx`, `HeroCta.tsx`,
`api/subscribe/route.ts` are untouched by main's rewrite) as a fresh change, and stop carrying the
branch as pending work.

**3. Pricing rule 2 still holds, still unguarded.** $20 bundle > $18 careers and > $9 CV, so the
careers report is not dominated. Rule 1 also holds — $29 mentor > $20 dearest product — **and
throws at import time** (`products.ts:110-122`), so it cannot ship broken. Rule 2 has no such
check and a **$2** margin. Unchanged for three weeks; now the longest-standing known trap in the
file, and a four-line `if` away from being as safe as rule 1.

**4. `leads.ts` normalises emails differently from `usage.ts`, so the dashboard can double-count
one person.** Low severity, flagged because step 8 is about exactly this. `usageKey()` writes
`fledgy:usage:<tool>:<normaliseEmail(email)>` — dots stripped for Gmail — while
`recordToolUse()` writes `fledgy:tools:<email.trim().toLowerCase()>`, un-normalised, and the tool
routes pass the raw address. For `a.b@gmail.com` the two key families therefore hold two different
strings for one person, and `getAllLeads()` adds both to its set. Result: two rows for one human,
one of them with an empty `tools` column, and an inflated lead count. **No mail is double-sent** —
`sendWhatsNew()` claims on `normaliseEmail(email)` — and `backfill.ts`'s `considered`/`remaining`
counts would be inflated but its sends would not. Cosmetic until someone counts leads and
believes the number. The fix is one call to `normaliseEmail` in `leads.ts`.
